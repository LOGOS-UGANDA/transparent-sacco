## The notice — Your privacy at [SACCO NAME]

**What we hold about you, who can see it, and what you can do about it.**

This page explains what information the SACCO keeps about you, who is allowed to look at it, and what your rights are. It is written to be read once, kept, and understood. If anything here is unclear, ask the SACCO office and we will explain it in person.

### What we keep about you

- Your name, national ID number (NIN), telephone number and membership number.
- Your savings balance and your share capital balance.
- Your loan history: what you applied for, what was approved, what you have repaid.
- For each deposit, the amount, the date, and the mobile money or bank reference.
- Records of who recorded and who approved each transaction.

We do not keep your mobile money PIN, your bank password, or copies of your bank statements.

### Why we keep it

So we can run your account, meet our legal duties as a cooperative, and let the people you elected check that the books are honest. We do not sell your information or share it with anyone outside the SACCO.

### Who can see your balance

Your balance is recorded privately. The public record holds only a sealed value — enough to prove your record is real, not enough to read the amount.

Who can see it:

- **You**, whenever you ask at the office.
- **The treasurer and the credit committee** — the officials you elected, who record deposits and approve loans. They hold the SACCO's shared key between them. No single one of them can use it alone.
- **The Supervisory Committee** — they can read, but they cannot change anything. Their key only lets them look.

**What this does and does not protect.** It protects you from the public, from people outside the SACCO, and from any one official acting alone. It does **not** hide your balance from the treasurer and committee themselves — their work requires them to see it. To find out who has looked at your record, ask the Supervisory Committee.

### What stays public

We would rather say this plainly than have you find out later. These are published on the public record:

- The SACCO's total savings and total share capital.
- The total value of loans disbursed and repaid.
- The total value of expenses, and what each expense was for.

The totals are kept as separate running counts, not as a list of members added up, so publishing them does not publish your balance. They are not, however, a secret: in a small SACCO with few members, knowing the total and everyone else's figure can hint at the last one. That is a known limit of publishing totals, and we would rather you knew it.

### Your rights

Under Uganda's **Data Protection and Privacy Act 2019**, you have the right to:

- **See** what we hold about you. Ask at the office with your ID and we will show you within 30 days.
- **Correct** anything that is wrong. If a deposit is recorded incorrectly, tell us and we will fix it and tell anyone we had already shared it with.
- **Ask us to stop** processing your information in a way that causes you serious distress. We must reply within 14 days.
- **Complain** to the National Information Technology Authority (NITA-U) if you think we have handled your information badly.

You may refuse to give us your information. If you do, we cannot keep a SACCO account for you — we cannot run an account without knowing whose it is.

### What happens if something goes wrong

If anyone gets access to your information without permission, we must report it to NITA-U immediately. If NITA-U decides you should be told, we will contact you by registered mail or email, and tell you enough to protect yourself.

### How long we keep it

We keep your records while you are a member, and for **[N] years** after you leave, because cooperative law and our auditors require it. Then we delete them.

**One honest limitation.** A record written to the public record cannot be individually deleted. Your balance is stored privately and can be removed, but the fact that a transaction happened at a certain time is permanent. You have a right to know that before you join, not after.
